# Core_Neural: Mathematical Specification

This document provides a cold, precise specification of the dynamical systems theory, state estimation, and spiking neural network formulations used within the `core_neural` engine.

---

## 1. Dimensionality Definitions

Let the continuous BCI space be defined by three discrete mathematical vectors:

1. **State Vector ($x_t \in \mathbb{R}^4$):**
   $$x_t = \begin{bmatrix} p_x \\ y_y \\ v_x \\ v_y \end{bmatrix}$$
   Where $p_x, p_y$ are 2D spatial coordinates (positions), and $v_x, v_y$ are corresponding velocity coordinates.

2. **Observation Input ($y_t \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$):**
   A real-valued spatial vector containing Local Field Potentials (LFPs) sampled across $64$ subdermal graphene micro-thread electrode channels.

3. **Spike Event Vector ($s_t \in \{0, 1\}^{32}$):**
   A sparse binary vector of neural events generated by $32$ event-driven leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) on-chip neuromorphic neurons.

---

## 2. Neuromorphic SNN Mechanics (Leaky Integrate-and-Fire)

Each spiking neuron $i \in \{0, \dots, 31\}$ integrates raw observation telemetry $y_t$ via synaptic weights $W_{\text{snn}} \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 64}$.

### Membrane Voltage Integration
$$V_i(t) = V_i(t-1) \cdot \lambda + \sum_{j=0}^{63} W_{\text{snn}}[i][j] \cdot y_t[j]$$

Where:
* $\lambda \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the membrane potential decay coefficient.
* $V_i(t)$ represents the membrane potential of neuron $i$ at time step $t$.

### Threshold and Reset Action
The spike activation function $s_t[i]$ and post-update voltage reset are governed by the threshold $V_{\text{th}} > 0$:

$$s_t[i] = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } V_i(t) \ge V_{\text{th}} \\
0 & \text{if } V_i(t) < V_{\text{th}}
\end{cases}$$

$$V_i(t) \leftarrow \begin{cases}
0.0 & \text{if } s_t[i] = 1 \\
V_i(t) & \text{if } s_t[i] = 0
\end{cases}$$

## 3. Kinematic State-Space Decoding

The kinematic estimate trajectory of the BCI system is updated as a linear discrete-time state-space transition equation:

$$x_{t+1} = A x_t + B s_t$$

Where:
* $A \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$ is the trajectory state transition matrix.
* $B \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ is the state control input mapping matrix, coupling binary spikes directly to kinetic acceleration profiles.

---

## 4. Contractive Stability & Convex Projection

To mathematically prevent state divergence, the decoder enforces strict contractive stability and parameter bounds.

### Contractive Stability Invariant
A system is contractive if any two trajectories $x^{(1)}_t$ and $x^{(2)}_t$ converge exponentially over time. Under a discrete-time discretization of step size $dt > 0$ and contractive decay coefficient $c > 0$, the contract is specified as:

$$\Vert{}x^{(1)}_{t+1} - x^{(2)}_{t+1}\Vert{}_2 \le (1 - c \cdot dt) \Vert{}x^{(1)}_t - x^{(2)}_t\Vert{}_2$$

Where $\Vert{}\cdot\Vert{}_2$ represents the standard Euclidean ($L_2$) vector norm.

### Convex Weight Projection
To guarantee that the state transition operator $A$ remains strictly contractive, its Frobenius norm $\Vert{}A\Vert{}_F$ is projected onto the convex constraint set $\mathcal{C}$ bounded by $W_{\max} > 0$:

$$\Vert{}A\Vert{}_F = \sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{3} \sum_{j=0}^{3} A[i][j]^2}$$

$$\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(A) = A \cdot \min\left(1, \frac{W_{\max}}{\Vert{}A\Vert{}_F}\right)$$
